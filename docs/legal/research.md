# Legal research for Ruken's pages

**Not legal advice.** This is a reading of public sources, done on 14 November 2026 to prepare drafts for a lawyer. A lawyer gives the final say (Owner, 2026-11-14). Where a point rests on a secondary source (a law firm's note, a vendor's guide), it says so; those should be checked against the law itself before anyone relies on them.

What Ruken is, for this reading (from the design and the code on `main`): a website run by Ruken App LLC, a US company. Anyone can watch game clips without an account. Playing needs a paid membership ($9.99 a month or $99 a year, renewing until cancelled, paid through Stripe Checkout, cancelled in Stripe's billing page). Accounts are for people 13 and up; the age is checked from a date of birth. Makers put games on Ruken and are paid a share through Stripe Connect (Express accounts, US banks only for now). Hosting is Cloudflare, the database Neon, mail Resend.

## 1. Children under 13 (COPPA)

- COPPA covers children under 13. The FTC's amended rule took effect 23 June 2025; the compliance date was 22 April 2026. [Federal Register, 22 April 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule) · [govinfo text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm)
- Ruken is a general-audience site with an age screen. The sign-up asks for a date of birth and keeps nothing typed by someone under 13. That is the usual way a general-audience site stays outside COPPA's consent duties.
- One gap: the FTC's long-standing COPPA guidance asks a neutral age screen to stop a child going back and typing an older date. Ruken keeps nothing after an under-13 answer, so nothing stops a second try. Stopping it would need a small necessary cookie or similar. **Open item for the Owner and the lawyer**; not built.

## 2. Members aged 13 to 17 paying

- COPPA does not reach 13 to 17. What does is ordinary contract law: in most US states a contract made by a minor can be undone by the minor (the "infancy" rule), usually with their money back, even after they used the service. Courts differ on how much must be handed back. [Cornell JLPP note on minors and app contracts](https://publications.lawschool.cornell.edu/jlpp/blogzine/minors-disaffirmance-of-digital-application-contracts-having-their-cake-and-eating-it-too/) · [T.K. v. Adobe (N.D. Cal. 2018), summary](https://www.studicata.com/summaries/united-states-district-court-northern-district-of-california/t-k-v-adobe-sys-inc-2018-h8frau) (California Family Code § 6710) · [Halbman v. Lemke, summary](https://www.lexplug.com/casebrief/halbman_v_lemke_65a2e0a13fc4374f9dad2c5b) (secondary sources).
- So a "no refunds" rule cannot stop a 13 to 17 year old from undoing the purchase. A parent's agreement makes the deal much safer, because the parent is an adult party.
- UK: the ICO's Children's Code covers services likely to be used by anyone under 18: high-privacy defaults, no profiling by default. [ICO, the code](https://ico.org.uk/media/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services-2-1.pdf) · [ICO FAQs on the 15 standards](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/faqs-on-the-15-standards-of-the-children-s-code/)
- EU: GDPR Article 8 sets an age (13 to 16 by country) for a child's own consent, but only where consent is the legal basis. Ruken's data use rests on the contract and on keeping accounts safe, not on consent, except the one optional cookie in the cookie spec.
- Lawful options (Owner question 3): (A) under 18s cannot pay; they can watch, and play anything free. On launch day the only game, FloorFifty, is free for everyone, so this costs nothing at launch. (B) under 18s pay only after a parent confirms by email. (C) under 18s tick "my parent or guardian says yes" at checkout. A is the simplest and safest; B is the usual long-term answer; C is the weakest. All three are later code, not built.

## 3. Renewal: what must be said, and reminders (US)

- **ROSCA** (federal law, 15 U.S.C. 8403): before charging, show all material terms clearly; get the person's express, informed consent; give a simple way to stop the charges. It is a statute, so it stands regardless of the FTC rule below.
- **The FTC's "click to cancel" rule** (2024 amendments to the Negative Option Rule) was set aside by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025 on procedure. Reports say the FTC restarted rule-making in 2026; the sources disagree on the steps, so treat the federal rule as uncertain and follow ROSCA and the states. [Jones Day, May 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses) (secondary).
- **California's automatic renewal law** (Business and Professions Code §§ 17600 to 17606), as amended by AB 390 (2022) and AB 2863 (from 1 July 2025): clear and conspicuous renewal terms next to the "buy" step; express affirmative consent to the renewal terms; an acknowledgement after purchase that the person can keep, with the terms and how to cancel; cancelling online if joined online; for a plan of one year or longer, a reminder 15 to 45 days before it renews; notice before a price change. [AB 2863 text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB2863/id/3012746) · [Kilpatrick Townsend note](https://ktslaw.com/en/insights/alert/2024/10/california-latest-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-in-july-2025) · [Mondaq on AB 390](https://mondaq.co.uk/unitedstates/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1186474/california-strengthens-its-automatic-renewal-law). Check the enacted § 17602 on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov; the sources differ on the new yearly notice.
- Many other states (New York, Colorado, Virginia, Illinois and others) have similar laws. Writing to California's rules covers most of them. [LAWYER TO CONFIRM]
- **What Ruken does today** (code on `main`): the join page says "Renews until you cancel. You can cancel any time from your account." next to the two prices; cancelling is in Stripe's billing page, online. **Gaps**: (1) no tick or button wording that is plainly agreeing to renewal; (2) no acknowledgement mail of our own (Stripe can send a receipt; it is a Stripe setting); (3) no reminder before a yearly renewal (Stripe Billing can send "upcoming renewal" mails; also a Stripe setting); (4) the join page does not say the renewal price and period in one sentence. Draft wording is in `terms.md`; building it is later work.

## 4. Refunds, the EU and UK 14 days, and card rules

- **No US law** requires refunds for a digital subscription in general. "No refunds" is allowed if it is shown before payment. Three exceptions: a minor undoing the deal (section 2); charges that should not have happened (after a cancel, or without proper renewal consent: ROSCA and California's law treat those as not agreed); and chargebacks.
- **Card networks**: a merchant may set its own refund policy, including none, but must show it before the purchase; in a dispute, Visa looks for proof that the buyer saw the terms and the cancellation and refund policy. A "no refunds" policy does not stop chargebacks. [Visa, cancelled services condition 13.7](https://usa.visa.com/supporting-info/cancelled-merchandise-services-condition-13-7.html) · [Stripe dispute guidance](https://support.stripe.com/questions/covid-19-dispute-response-guidance) · [Payrix disclosure guide](https://resource.payrix.com/docs/e-commerce-disclosure-requirements.md) (secondary).
- **Stripe** asks every business to show its refund and cancellation policy, its contact details and its terms on its site. [LAWYER TO CONFIRM against Stripe's current services agreement]
- **EU** (Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83): a consumer who buys online has 14 days to withdraw. For a service, starting early at the consumer's request means they pay for the days used if they withdraw (Article 14(3)); the right ends only when the service is fully performed (Article 16(a)). The digital-content exception (Article 16(m)) needs express consent and an acknowledgement, confirmed on a durable medium. A clause that waives these rights is not binding (Article 25). [Article 16](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/16) · [Chapter III](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/chapter/III). Reported: in Sky Österreich (C-234/25, July 2026) the Court of Justice held a streaming subscription is a digital service, not digital content, so the 16(m) exception does not apply to it. [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/consumer-protection/cjeu-decides-when-streaming-subscriptions-are-subject-to-the-right-of-withdrawal/) (secondary; not checked against the judgment). A membership to play games is likely treated the same way.
- **EU withdrawal button**: from 19 June 2026, an online seller must offer a two-step "withdraw from contract here" function where a withdrawal right exists (Directive (EU) 2023/2673, new Article 11a). [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-consumer-protection-law-update-new-9377759/) · [Arnold & Porter, May 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/05/eu-withdrawal-button-uk-subscription-rules-and-data-protection-risks-for-us-online-sellers) (secondary).
- **UK**: the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 give the same 14 days, with the same "pay for what you used" rule for services. The DMCC Act 2024's subscription rules (reminders, cooling-off at each renewal) are reported to start in 2027 (January or spring; sources differ). [Lewis Silkin, April 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/04/02/consumer-law-update-subscriptions-regime-delayed-again-to-spring-2027-102mops) · [Brodies](https://brodies.com/insights/commercial-contracts-and-outsourcing/the-dmcca-subscription-contracts-the-timeline-just-got-shorter/) (secondary).
- **So**: "no refunds" works for US members who are adults and were charged properly. It does not work for EU or UK consumers, for minors, or for a charge that should not have happened. See the conflicts list (section 13).

## 5. Cookies and consent before collecting data (EU, UK)

- **EU ePrivacy Directive, Article 5(3)**: storing or reading anything on a person's device needs their consent, except what is strictly necessary to provide the service they asked for (or to carry a communication). The EDPB reads this widely: it covers cookies, local storage, pixels and scripts that read from the device. [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023, version 2](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf) · [Hunton summary](https://huntonak.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/edpb-publishes-guidelines-to-clarify-scope-of-eu-cookie-notice-and-consent-requirements)
- **What counts as strictly necessary** (Article 29 Working Party Opinion 04/2012 on the cookie consent exemption, which the EDPB and the national regulators still follow): a sign-in cookie for the session, a security cookie (for example against repeated failed sign-ins), load balancing, a cookie that remembers a person's cookie choice, and user-interface choices the person made. Not exempt: advertising, analytics from third parties, and tracking someone across sites. A cookie that remembers which affiliate or referrer sent a visitor is generally not strictly necessary to the visitor. [LAWYER TO CONFIRM for Ruken's "which maker brought you" cookie]
- **Server logs and IP addresses** are not storage on the device, so Article 5(3) does not cover them; GDPR does. The legal basis for keeping a sign-in record, a short security log or a rate-limit counter is the contract (Article 6(1)(b)) or a legitimate interest in security (Article 6(1)(f), and recital 49 names network and information security). Consent is not needed, and is not the right basis, for these.
- **UK**: PECR regulation 6, with the same strictly-necessary exemption. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 added exemptions for statistics, site appearance and function, and emergencies, in force 5 February 2026; the ICO's final guidance on "storage and access technologies" explains them. [Brodies on the ICO guidance](https://brodies.com/insights/ip-technology-and-data/storage-and-access-technologies-ico-publishes-updated-guidance-on-the-use-of-cookies-and-other-tracking-technologies/) · [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/new-exceptions-explained-in-ico-final-guidance-on-storage-and-access-technologies-could-online-advertising-be-next/) (secondary).
- **US**: no general law requires cookie consent. The Owner's rule ("we can't collect any data AT ALL until that's approved") is stricter than US law, and in one way stricter than any law can be met: see `cookie-consent-spec.md`, "What cannot wait for a yes".

## 6. Privacy rights: EU, UK and California

- **GDPR and UK GDPR**: they reach a US company that offers services to people in the EU or UK (Article 3(2)). People have the right to be told what is kept (Articles 13 and 14), to a copy, to correction, to deletion, to restriction, to portability, and to object; answers are due within one month (Article 12(3)). Identity may be checked when there is reasonable doubt (Article 12(6)). A non-EU controller must name a representative in the EU (Article 27) unless its processing is occasional and low-risk. The privacy page must give the controller's identity and contact details; an email address is the usual minimum. [LAWYER TO CONFIRM whether Ruken needs a representative, and whether an email alone is enough contact detail]
- **EU Digital Services Act**: a hosting service (Ruken hosts makers' games and pages) that offers services in the EU must give a point of contact for users and authorities (Articles 11 and 12), a way to report illegal content (Article 16), and reasons when it removes something (Article 17). A provider with no EU establishment must name a legal representative in the EU (Article 13); the small-company exemptions do not seem to cover Article 13. [Prighter, DSA representative](https://prighter.com/resources/dsa-legal-representative) · [Taylor Wessing overview](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/interface/2022/the-eus-digital-services-act/digital-services-act---an-overview) (secondary).
- **California (CCPA as amended by CPRA)**: it applies to a business with over $26,625,000 in yearly gross revenue (the figure for 2025 and 2026), or that buys, sells or shares the personal information of 100,000 or more Californians, or makes half its revenue from selling or sharing it. [CCPA thresholds, DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/09/ccpa-2025-updated-regulations) · [Clym guide](https://www.clym.io/blog/ccpa-applicability-guide) (secondary). Ruken at launch is unlikely to meet any of the three. Ruken does not sell or share personal information for ads, so no "Do Not Sell or Share" link is needed even when it does apply; the privacy page says "We do not sell your data" (Owner, 2026-11-14). [LAWYER TO CONFIRM]
- **California's "Shine the Light" and § 1789.3**: see section 10.
- **Other US states** (Texas, Virginia, Colorado and others) have privacy laws with their own thresholds; most would not apply at launch. [LAWYER TO CONFIRM]

## 7. Sales tax and VAT

- **US sales tax**: since South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018), a state can require a remote seller to collect sales tax once it passes that state's "economic nexus" threshold (often $100,000 of sales, some with a count of transactions). The seller also owes tax in its own home state if the product is taxable there. Whether a digital games subscription is taxable differs by state. [Stripe, sales tax applicability](https://stripe.com/resources/more/sales-tax-applicability) · [Stripe, do I need to charge sales tax](https://stripe.com/resources/more/do-i-need-to-charge-sales-tax-in-the-us)
- **Who is the seller**: Ruken. Members buy a membership from Ruken App LLC; makers are paid a share by Ruken. Ruken is not collecting money for makers' own sales.
- **Stripe Tax**: Ruken's checkout turns it on (`automatic_tax: { enabled: true }` in `src/features/membership/checkout.ts`). Stripe Tax only charges tax where a registration has been added in Stripe; with none, it charges nothing. Its threshold monitoring is advice only ("it's up to you to confirm whether registration is actually required"), counts only Stripe sales, and only in live mode. Stripe can register on Ruken's behalf for a fee. [Stripe Tax monitoring](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring) · [Use Stripe to register](https://edge-docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register)
- **EU and UK VAT**: a non-EU business selling digital services to EU consumers owes VAT from the first sale; there is no threshold. It can register once, in one EU country, under the non-Union One Stop Shop. The UK is the same, with registration from the first sale. [EU OSS guidelines](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2021-07/OSS_guidelines_en.pdf) · [Irish Revenue, non-Union scheme](https://revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/value-added-tax/part10-special-schemes/vat-ecommerce-rules/non-union-scheme-one-stop-shop-oss.pdf)
- **What to do before launch** [ACCOUNTANT OR LAWYER TO CONFIRM]: (1) register for sales tax in Ruken App LLC's home state if a games subscription is taxable there, and add that registration in Stripe Tax; (2) watch Stripe's threshold monitoring monthly and register where crossed; (3) either register for EU and UK VAT before taking money from people there, or take memberships from the US only at launch (Owner question 4). This is the Owner's to do; the pages only say "Tax is added where it applies."

## 8. Makers' tax forms

- Ruken pays makers through Stripe Connect **Express** accounts. For Express accounts the platform, not Stripe, is responsible for filing and delivering 1099 forms; Stripe gives the tools, but the platform must start the filing before the deadline. [Stripe Connect tax reporting](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/tax-reporting) · [Stripe support, 1099 for platforms](https://support.stripe.com/questions/1099-forms-for-platforms-and-marketplaces) · [Express dashboard taxes](https://docs.stripe.com/docs/connect/platform-express-dashboard-taxes)
- Stripe collects the maker's tax details (the W-9 information) during Connect onboarding when the platform asks for them. [Required verification information for taxes](https://docs.stripe.com/docs/connect/required-verification-information-taxes)
- **Which form** [ACCOUNTANT OR LAWYER TO CONFIRM]: makers license their games to Ruken and get a share of fees, which looks like a royalty (Form 1099-MISC, box 2, from $10 a year) more than pay for services (1099-NEC). The One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised the 1099-NEC and most 1099-MISC thresholds to $2,000 from tax year 2026 (royalties stay at $10) and set 1099-K back to $20,000 and 200 transactions. [Avalara on OBBBA](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html) · [TaxBandits, 2026 changes](https://www.taxbandits.com/irs-updates/form-1099-changes-2026/) (secondary). 1099-K is for payment settlement entities; Ruken is not settling makers' own sales, so 1099-K is unlikely to be Ruken's form.
- **Makers outside the US** (not possible yet: US banks only): they would give a W-8BEN, and US royalties paid to them can carry 30% withholding unless a treaty lowers it.
- The first statement is 10 December and the first payout 15 December 2026, so the first 1099s would be for tax year 2026, due early 2027.

## 9. Apple, if Ruken is ever wrapped as an app or listed

Ruken is a website. Apple's App Store rules do not apply to a website opened in Safari. They would apply if Ruken were wrapped in an iOS app. Source for all: [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (check the live text; the search results were mostly summaries).

- **3.1.1**: digital content or a membership unlocked inside an app must be sold with Apple's in-app purchase. A US court order in Epic v. Apple (2025) lets US apps link out to buy on the web; how far that reaches is still moving. [LAWYER TO CONFIRM at the time]
- **4.2**: an app that is only a website in a wrapper can be refused.
- **4.7**: HTML5 games inside an app are allowed with conditions, and the host app is responsible for them. The November 2025 revision names HTML5 and JavaScript mini games. [Apple developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=7j1f99yf)
- **4.8**: an app that offers a third-party sign-in (Google is planned after launch) must also offer an equivalent private sign-in option, such as Sign in with Apple.
- **5.1.1(v)**: an app that lets people make an account must let them start deleting it inside the app. Ruken already has "Delete my account". [Apple, offering account deletion](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app)
- **Age ratings**: an app would need an age rating that fits "13 and up".
- **Safari and WebKit**: Safari blocks third-party cookies and limits storage written by scripts. Ruken's cookies are first-party and set by the server, so they are not affected. Games run in a frame on another address, so a game's saved progress in Safari is kept per site and can be cleared. [WebKit tracking prevention](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/)
- Apple has no rules a website must follow to be opened in Safari or added to the Home Screen.

## 10. Who we are, and addresses

- **CAN-SPAM** (15 U.S.C. 7704; 16 CFR 316): a commercial email must carry a valid physical postal address: a street address, a USPS PO box, or a private mailbox at a registered commercial mail agency. Transactional or relationship messages (sign-in links, password resets, receipts, account notices) are outside most of these rules. [16 CFR part 316, eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-316) · [FTC 2008 rule on PO boxes](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2008-05-21/html/E8-11394.htm). Ruken's mails today (confirm your email, reset your password, your password was changed, sign-in paused) are all transactional. The wishlist mail "a game you asked about is out" is probably commercial. [LAWYER TO CONFIRM]
- **California Civil Code § 1789.3**: a provider of an electronic commercial service must tell California consumers, when they sign up and each year by 30 June, its name, address and telephone number, its charges, and how to complain to the Department of Consumer Affairs' Complaint Assistance Unit. [§ 1789.3](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1789.3) · [California DCA legal guide M-1](https://www.dca.ca.gov/publications/legal_guides/m_1.shtml)
- **EU Consumer Rights Directive, Article 6(1)(c)**: before an online sale, the trader gives its geographical address and contact details.
- **DMCA safe harbour** (17 U.S.C. 512(c)): a site that hosts what others upload (makers' games) is protected from copyright claims only if it has a designated agent registered with the US Copyright Office, with contact details on its site. The registration asks for a mailing address. [LAWYER TO CONFIRM; this was not on the Owner's list and matters for "don't get sued"]

## 11. Governing law and disputes

- A US consumer contract may choose the law of the company's home state and its courts, and may have arbitration. EU and UK consumers keep the protection of their own country's law and can sue at home whatever the contract says (Rome I Article 6; Brussels I bis Article 18).
- **Proposal** [LAWYER TO CONFIRM]: the law of the state where Ruken App LLC is formed (Owner question 6); disputes go to that state's courts, or to small claims court anywhere it is allowed; a 30-day "talk to us first" step. **No arbitration clause for now**, because consumer arbitration rules make the company pay most of the fees, and thousands of filings at once ("mass arbitration") can cost more than a court case. A lawyer may prefer arbitration with a class-action waiver; that is a trade they should make.

## 12. Liability

- US law allows "as is" wording and a cap on damages in consumer terms, within limits (no cap on fraud, gross negligence, or what a statute forbids). EU and UK law forbid excluding liability for death or injury caused by negligence and limit one-sided terms (Unfair Terms Directive 93/13; UK Consumer Rights Act 2015, Part 2).
- **Proposal** [LAWYER TO CONFIRM]: the service and the games are "as is"; Ruken is not responsible for makers' games; total liability is capped at what the person paid Ruken in the 12 months before the claim, or $50 if they paid nothing; with "nothing here limits rights you have by law that cannot be limited."

## 14. What only the Owner can answer
