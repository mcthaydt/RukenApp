**R8. L's length rule gives wrong words for common files.**
UNIT: 2.7 (L). VERDICT: red. REQUIREMENT: 2.7 and 9 ("the words in this file are the words shipped"). REASON: (a) a fragmented MP4 (what Safari's and some recorders' MP4 is) has `mvhd` duration 0; "duration ÷ timescale" reads 0 and the maker is told "That video is 0 seconds". (b) The rule is "measured to the tenth" but the words show whole seconds, so a 14.95-second video is refused as "That video is 15 seconds. Videos need to be 15 to 60 seconds long." (c) WebM's `TimecodeScale` defaults to 1,000,000 ns when absent; 2.7 does not say so. (d) The browser reports `Infinity` or `NaN` for a WebM with no length, or nothing for a codec it cannot play. EVIDENCE: 2.7's bullets. EXPECTED: a real 30-second video is never refused, and refusal words never show a length inside the range. FIX: 2.7 says: `mvhd` duration 0 or all ones means read `mehd`, and if that is absent the length is unknown (the "couldn't tell" words); compare the exact seconds with 15 and 60; too short shows the seconds rounded down, too long rounded up; `TimecodeScale` defaults to 1,000,000 ns; in the browser a length that is not a finite number refuses nothing. L's step 1 adds a fragmented MP4 fixture and 14.95 and 60.04 seconds. Only 2.7 and L change.
