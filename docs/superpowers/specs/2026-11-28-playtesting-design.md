## Rules

- **One row per person and game,** `Playtester { id, gameId, userId, state, via, askedAt?, decidedAt?, takenBackAt?, noticeAt?, noticeSeenAt?, noticeGoneAt? }`, `@@unique([gameId, userId])`. `state` is `asked | in | declined | removed`; `via` is `ask | invite | link`. Ask: creates `asked`; on the caller's own `declined` row it clears `takenBackAt` and the row stays `declined`; on any other row it does nothing. Take back: deletes the caller's own `asked` row; on a `declined` row it only sets `takenBackAt` (the page shows "Ask to playtest" again), so a no is never undone by the player. Asking newest first sorts by `askedAt`, which only a new row sets. Yes: `asked → in`. No: `asked → declined`. Invite: creates `in` with `via = invite`, or turns `asked`/`declined` into `in` and sets `via = invite` (so Playing says "Invited" and Your turn says "invited you"); a `removed` row turns back to `in` only by a maker's invite (the maker changed their mind), never by the link, and that re-invite sets `via = invite` and a new `decidedAt` too. A link open makes `in` with `via = link` from no row, and turns an `asked` row into `in` with `via = link` (a yes); on a `declined`, `in` or `removed` row it does nothing, so the link never undoes a no or a removal. A link open sets no `noticeAt`, so it makes no Your turn row (the person is already on the game's page). Remove: `in → removed`, and it sets `noticeGoneAt` so the Your turn row leaves the list. The player's own Remove on the row sets `noticeGoneAt` too (nothing else). A yes or invite sets `noticeAt` (the row's time and the dot) only when the row has never had one; an invite on a `removed` row clears `noticeGoneAt` and keeps `noticeAt` and `noticeSeenAt`, so the row comes back at its old time and the dot stays off once seen. A row the player hid comes back too when the maker removes and invites them again (the maker did something new); the pull request names this. A playtest row shows in Your turn when `state = in`, `noticeAt` is set, `noticeGoneAt` is null, the game is live, it is before launch, and (for an 18+ game) the reader is known to be 18; it lights the dot when it shows and `noticeSeenAt` is null. Seen (`noticeSeenAt`) is set for every shown playtest row by the same one post that marks sends seen. Take back then Ask on an `asked` row makes a new row and moves the player to the top of Asking; a no still sticks, so this is left with the deferred ask limit and named in the pull request. Every change is one guarded `updateMany` on the expected state, so two presses or two tabs never do it twice. Times come from the injected clock.
- **Who may change what.** Only the game's maker answers, invites, removes, flips the switch and makes a link; only the asker takes back their own ask. The maker never asks for or plays-as-playtester their own game. A person the maker blocked from sending (a `SendStop` either way) can still ask; nothing here reads it. Anything else answers 404 and changes nothing. An ask or link open for a game that `adultsOnlyRefusal` refuses for the caller answers as Wishlist does and changes nothing. An invite is made for every friend, whatever their age, exactly as a send is written for every friend and hidden at read time (`src/features/sends/service.ts:122`): the sheet ticks "Invited Mia." and Mia shows under Playing, so nothing the maker sees depends on the friend's age. All hiding is on the friend's side, judged on the friend as the reader: the Your turn row and the dot leave out an 18+ game for someone not known to be 18, and the game's page and `/g/<game>/play` refuse them with 403 through `adultsOnlyRefusal`, as they do today. The Your turn rows and the dot take the reader's own `isAdult`, as the sends do. Pages carry no internal ids a person can change to reach someone else's row.
- **Friends** are Your turn's friends (`canSend` and the friend list in `src/features/sends/`); an invite to someone who is not a friend is refused (404).
- **Who may play.** `playBasis` gains `playtest`, checked after own game, staff and free game, and before member: so a member's playtest play is a `playtest` play and never counts toward the pool. It holds only for a row in state `in`, on a live game, before launch. The play route reads it with the membership check (`isPlaytester` on the viewer), so `play` does not import the new feature. `money` and the feed's play counts read `member` (and `free_game`) only, so they need no change; a database test proves a `playtest` session is left out of the pool's roll-up.
- **The link** is 128 random bits, base64url, kept as the token itself in `PlaytestLink { gameId @id, token @unique, madeAt }`, so the maker's sheet shows the same link every time it opens. Only the maker's page reads it; it gives nothing but a playtest place on one game, and anyone who can read the database can already change every row, so a hash buys nothing here. A new link replaces the row, and the old token then matches nothing.
- **What is kept, and deletion.** Who asked to playtest which game, the answer and when, and whether they came by an ask, an invite or the link (only the maker invites, so no inviter is kept); the link. Rows are kept until the account (or the maker's account) is deleted. Deleting an account deletes every `Playtester` row it is in as a player and, with the maker's games, every row and their `PlaytestLink` rows, in `deletion.ts`, before the purge's other steps that need them gone. `/privacy` says all of this, how long rows are kept included, with its "Last updated" date. One new cookie, `rk_playtest_pressed` (30 minutes, names the game, like `rk_wish_pressed`), listed in `src/features/consent/cookies.ts` and on `/privacy`; no browser-kept value. `/about` is not changed (the owner did not ask for it).
- **Cookie consent.** `/p/<token>`, the ask and take-back routes and every `/make/…/playtesters` action keep something, so they are `GATED` in `src/features/consent/routes.ts`; the game page and feed stay as they are.
- **Later feed pages.** Clips past the first page come from `/api/feed`, so the feed's loads pass the viewer's playtest state for all games once (which games show this viewer the badge, that is the switch is on and the viewer is not its maker and has no `in` or `removed` row; and which the viewer plays), as Wishlist passes the whole list, and later clips show the badge and Play correctly.
- **Gamepad and keyboard.** Every new control is a real button or link and reachable by the gamepad layer and Tab, with visible focus only from keyboard or gamepad (`:focus-visible`).
- **No overlap.** The badge, the band's extra line and the Playtesters page pass the standing overlap journey at 320×568, 375×667 and 667×375, and every control is at least 48px.
- **Words.** All new words in one file, `src/features/playtest/words.ts`, so the screens and the journeys read the same ones. `sends` sits below `playtest` and cannot import it, so `src/routes/your-turn/+page.server.ts` builds each playtest row's line from `playtest/words.ts` and `YourTurn.svelte` takes that line as data (a send's line it still builds itself from `SEND_WORDS`). No "beta", no "tester" jargon beyond "playtest" and "playtester".

## The state table

This table is the source of truth for every person-and-game state. Where the prose above differs, the table wins. One table-driven database test in `tests/features/playtest/playtest.pg.test.ts` ("state table") seeds every state and runs every action, and checks each cell's result (the row's fields after the action, and what the service returns). The cells marked J are also walked in the journey `tests/browser/playtest.test.ts`.

The states are for one person P and one game G that is live, before launch, with the switch on, unless a column says otherwise.
- **none:** no row, and P is not G's maker.
- **asked:** `state = asked`.
- **declined:** `state = declined`, `takenBackAt` null. P sees "You asked…".
- **taken back:** `state = declined`, `takenBackAt` set. P sees "Ask to playtest".
- **in:** `state = in`.
- **removed:** `state = removed`.
- **maker:** P is G's maker. They never have a row.

"Nothing" means no field changes. The answer is the usual page, or 404 where the row says so.
